# Research

## Context
This project explores **how collaborative data visualization environments can enable diverse audiences to address complex, wicked problems**. Wicked problems—such as climate change mitigation, peacebuilding, and social inequalities—are complex challenges without clear definitions or solutions. Addressing them requires collaboration among people with varied expertise and interests, including researchers, policymakers, business leaders, educators, and citizens. By working together, these groups can better understand the problems and co-create potential solutions. However, tools and platforms are needed to enable effective collaboration.

Data provides the evidence needed for understanding and informed decision-making. Relevant data can come from top-down sources, such as satellite images or computer model projections, and from bottom-up sources, like polls, opinions, and community-collected information. While data visualizations are vital for making sense of this information, **existing tools are often designed for specific user groups**, such as domain experts, often following a top-down approach. As a result, much data remains inaccessible to broader audiences, limiting opportunities for informed collaboration, participation, and education. 

## Goals

CollEcTIVE aims to **empower people to collaboratively tackle wicked problems using innovative data visualization environments**. 
- We **study how people currently collaborate with data** and how such collaboration enhances understanding, learning, and problem-solving. 
- We **design, develop, and evaluate user interfaces** that support collaborative data-centered activities, including data collection and sharing, exploration and visualization of complex datasets, communication of findings, deliberation, and communicating solutions.

## Approach
We will focus on two complementary approaches to support collaboration:
- **Co-located environments** to explore situated visualizations, interactive physicalizations, and immersive experiences that allow people to meet in person and engage with data. The goal is to study how data can be actively integrated into face-to-face collaboration, so people can use them for understanding and deliberation.
- **Online platforms** to connect individual co-located sessions and enable continuous, ongoing collaboration though this platform. 

## Deliveries:
- New **theoretical understanding** of data-centered collaboration for wicked problems.
- Innovative **user interfaces, environments, and platforms** to support both co-located and online data-driven collaboration.
Use cases illustrating collaboration scenarios within these environments.
- **Empirical evidence** for the effectiveness of these platforms, and insights into which user interface design choices most effectively support successful collaboration.
